# Batch 3 — cultural burning

44 frames, a **cultural burning shoot** — fire-stick farming on Turraburra, from reading the
grass before a burn through to burnt ground already regenerating.
Masters 4096 × 3072 (39 landscape, 5 portrait), WebP q82, 38 MB.
Figma: `PHOTO LIBRARY - 3rd batch`, node `2756:34661`, children `2756:34617`–`34660`.
Local: `brand/photography/batch-3/`.

**Resolution: 2.84× against a 1440 viewport — the best headroom in the collection**
(batch 1 is 2.7×, batch 2 is 1.39×). Every frame clears the 1.5× Ken Burns floor except
`fire-slope-lowres`, which is 1024 × 768 and clears nothing.

## Why these are renamed, and how the names work

The frames arrived as iPhone export UUIDs — `3FD40A13-624E-41DD-A748-4CBB0E28DB97_1_105_c`.
Forty-one characters, no ordering, no information, and impossible to put in a table cell in
`PHOTO-INDEX.md`, which exists to make choosing a photograph fast. Batches 1 and 2 kept their
camera-native names because those names were short and ordered; a UUID gives up everything
except provenance.

**The slug says what is visibly in the frame, and nothing else.** The first token is the
dominant physical subject — `fire`, `smoke`, `burn`, `shelter`, `hands`, `crew`, `aerial`,
`country` — then a qualifier, then `-tall` if the frame is portrait. A slug never makes an
interpretive or cultural claim: there is no `ceremony`, no `sacred`, no `ritual` in this
scheme, because that judgement belongs to Our People and not to a filename.

**The risk this takes on, stated plainly.** `PHOTO-INDEX.md` says every frame was described
from the image itself and nothing inferred from a filename. A descriptive slug puts an
inference into the path, where it outlives the description. The **Origin** line on every entry
is what keeps that reversible — it carries the UUID and the Figma node, and since
`brand/photography/` is gitignored **this file is the only durable record of that mapping.**
An entry without an Origin line has lost it.

The renaming happened once, at ingest, before anything referenced a file. Renaming a master
that already has references is a different act and is not permitted — see `brand/README.md`.

The Figma layer names were renamed to match, so the board and this file agree.

## Read this before picking anything from batch 3

**Fire on this site is fire-stick farming — a practice and a responsibility, not weather and
not atmosphere.** Embers as sparkle behind an unrelated headline trivialises it. That rule
predates this batch (`sketch-library.md`); forty-four photographs now stand behind it. A fire
frame belongs beside copy about caring for Country, and nowhere else.

**The trap in this batch is beauty.** Several frames are the most spectacular pictures in the
whole collection — a burn line under an orange sunset, a figure against a wall of flame at
night. They will be reached for as heroes on pages that have nothing to do with fire. Don't.

**Four frames carry the sandstone shelter itself** — `shelter-worker-seated`,
`shelter-fire-foreground`, `shelter-ledge-walking` and `shelter-seedling`. Four more have
sandstone outcrop or escarpment in frame and are flagged on their entries:
`fire-ignition-outcrop`, `burn-regrowth-ledge`, `burn-escarpment-shadow` and
`burn-track-outcrop-tall`. All eight are bucketed `cultural-site`, not `work`, even though the activity in them is the same as in the frames beside them. **The sensitive
element in frame decides the bucket, not the activity**, and anything ambiguous goes to the
more restrictive bucket.

**No children appear in any frame in this batch.** Stated rather than left silent, because
silence is not the same answer.

**Three frames are crowds.** `crew-group-portrait` is a group photograph of around
twenty-five adults, most faces identifiable — **the largest single consent block in the
project**, larger than batch 1's eight. `crew-demonstration` shows around eighteen people
watching a demonstration burn, also identifiable. `aerial-crew-burnedge` shows around fifteen
from a drone, where nobody is identifiable at all — which makes it the safe way to show a crew.

**Five frames are portrait** — `burn-sun-heath-tall`, `smoke-canopy-tall`,
`burn-track-outcrop-tall`, `fire-stags-tall`, `fire-trunk-night-tall`. `ASSETS.md` §10 gap 9
records that batch 1 is 100% landscape and mobile heroes have nothing tall to work with.
These are the first portrait frames of Country in the collection.

**Three frames are aerial** — `country-wide-track`, `aerial-crew-burnedge`,
`aerial-burn-scar`. They show tracks across the property. No coordinates, place names or boundaries may be added in captions, alt text or map data.
